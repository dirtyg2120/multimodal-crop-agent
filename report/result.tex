\section{Evaluation Design}

The evaluation follows a three-track strategy, summarised in
Table~\ref{tab:eval_strategy}.
Both CLIP (\texttt{Keetawan/clip-vit-large-patch14-plant-disease-finetuned}~\cite{keetawan2024plantdisease})
and ResNet-50 (\texttt{SanketJadhav/PlantDiseaseClassifier-Resnet50}~\cite{SanketJadhavplantdisease}) are
fine-tuned on PlantVillage~\cite{DBLP:journals/corr/HughesS15}, ensuring the comparison isolates architectural
capability rather than data advantage.

\begin{table}[h]
    \centering
    \caption{Three-track evaluation strategy.}
    \label{tab:eval_strategy}
    \begin{tabular}{p{1.2cm}p{2.5cm}p{3cm}p{3cm}p{4cm}}
        \hline
        \textbf{Exp.} & \textbf{Dataset} & \textbf{Models} & \textbf{Metrics} & \textbf{What It Proves} \\
        \hline
        1 & PlantVillage & CLIP vs ResNet-50 & Top-1 Accuracy & Closed-set comparison on equal footing \\
        \hline
        2 & PlantVillage & CLIP threshold sweep & Unknown F1, Rejection Rate & Open-set rejection; CLIP better calibrated \\
        \hline
        3 & PlantVillage & Few-Shot Engine & TPR, FPR, F1 & Prototype-based unknown registration at inference time \\
        \hline
        4 & PlantDoc & Full DINO + CLIP pipeline & Qualitative case analysis & End-to-end pipeline robustness \\
        \hline
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\section{Experiment 1: Closed-Set Classification Accuracy}
\label{sec:exp1}


Both classifiers were evaluated on 31 PlantVillage disease classes
(Apple, Corn, Grape, Orange, Pepper, Potato, Strawberry, Tomato)
with $\tau = 0$ (no rejection) and 150~images per class (4,650 total).
CLIP uses \texttt{ViT-L/14}~\cite{keetawan2024plantdisease};
ResNet-50 uses a 38-class fine-tuned checkpoint~\cite{SanketJadhavplantdisease}.


\begin{table}[h]
    \centering
    \caption{Closed-set classification metrics (31 classes, 4,650 samples, $\tau=0$).}
    \label{tab:closed_set}
    \begin{tabular}{lccc}
        \hline
        \textbf{Model} & \textbf{Top-1 Accuracy (\%)} & \textbf{Macro-F1} & \textbf{Weighted-F1} \\
        \hline
        CLIP (ViT-L/14, fine-tuned) & \textbf{99.97} & \textbf{1.00} & \textbf{1.00} \\
        ResNet-50 (fine-tuned)      & 95.18          & 0.82          & 0.95          \\
        \hline
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

CLIP achieves near-perfect accuracy across all 31 classes.
ResNet-50 shows meaningful degradation on visually similar Tomato diseases
(Table~\ref{tab:closed_set_perclass}); the macro-F1 of 0.82 is depressed by
zero-support classes in ResNet's 38-class output space --- the weighted-F1
of 0.95 is the more informative aggregate.

\begin{table}[h]
    \centering
    \caption{Per-class F1 for the most challenging ResNet-50 classes.}
    \label{tab:closed_set_perclass}
    \begin{tabular}{lcc}
        \hline
        \textbf{Class} & \textbf{CLIP F1} & \textbf{ResNet-50 F1} \\
        \hline
        Tomato Early Blight                        & 1.00 & 0.76 \\
        Corn Cercospora Leaf Spot                  & 0.99 & 0.84 \\
        Tomato Target Spot                         & 1.00 & 0.82 \\
        Tomato Bacterial Spot                      & 0.99 & 0.89 \\
        Corn Northern Leaf Blight                  & 0.99 & 0.87 \\
        Tomato Late Blight                         & 1.00 & 0.90 \\
        Healthy Tomato leaf                        & 1.00 & 0.95 \\
        \hline
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The largest gap is on \textit{Tomato Early Blight}: ResNet-50 recall is 63\%
(F1 = 0.76) versus CLIP's 100\%, indicating that CLIP's vision-language
alignment provides stronger discriminative features for fine-grained lesion
patterns.
Both models were evaluated on the same distribution they were trained on,
so the near-perfect CLIP scores reflect architectural capability on the
training domain, not data leakage; the purpose of Experiment~1 is to confirm
that open-set capability (Experiments 2 and~3) does not degrade closed-set
performance.

\section{Experiment 2: CLIP Confidence Threshold Sensitivity}
\label{sec:exp2}

A mixed dataset of 10 known classes (2 Grape, 8 Tomato) and 3 unknown classes
(\textit{Squash Powdery Mildew}, \textit{Raspberry healthy},
\textit{Blueberry healthy}) was constructed from PlantVillage.
Potato was excluded as a candidate unknown due to nightshade family overlap
with CLIP pre-training data.
50~images per class were sampled per run (44 for Tomato Mosaic Virus),
giving 644~images per run across 5 independent runs.
Thresholds $\tau \in [0.30, 0.95]$ were swept in steps of 0.05.
Unknown F1 is the harmonic mean of unknown precision
($\text{TP}/(\text{TP}+\text{FP})$) and unknown recall
($\text{TP}/(\text{TP}+\text{FN})$), where a slip-through (unknown accepted
as a known label) counts as a false negative.

\begin{table}[h]
    \centering
    \caption{CLIP threshold sensitivity (mean over 5 runs, 10 known + 3 unknown
             classes). Best Unknown F1 highlighted in bold.}
    \label{tab:clip_threshold}
    \begin{tabular}{ccccc}
        \hline
        \textbf{$\tau$} & \textbf{Known Acc.\ (\%)} & \textbf{Unk.\ Rej.\ (\%)} & \textbf{False Rej.\ (\%)} & \textbf{Unk.\ F1} \\
        \hline
        0.30 & 25.7 & 17.1 & 13.0 & 0.213 \\
        0.35 & 24.9 & 25.9 & 17.3 & 0.282 \\
        0.40 & 24.3 & 34.4 & 23.2 & 0.326 \\
        0.45 & 23.8 & 40.4 & 27.3 & 0.351 \\
        0.50 & 22.8 & 45.6 & 31.1 & 0.367 \\
        0.55 & 21.9 & 49.6 & 34.5 & 0.377 \\
        0.60 & 20.7 & 54.0 & 38.0 & 0.387 \\
        0.65 & 20.4 & 59.6 & 42.0 & 0.400 \\
        0.70 & 19.7 & 61.6 & 45.5 & 0.396 \\
        0.75 & 19.0 & 66.7 & 49.5 & 0.405 \\
        0.80 & 18.1 & 70.8 & 53.2 & 0.409 \\
        \textbf{0.85} & \textbf{17.0} & \textbf{75.5} & \textbf{56.1} & \textbf{0.419} \\
        0.90 & 15.1 & 78.7 & 60.8 & 0.415 \\
        0.95 & 14.1 & 82.1 & 65.7 & 0.412 \\
        \hline
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Unknown F1 peaks at $\tau^* = 0.85$ (F1 = 0.419) and is flat between
$\tau = 0.75$ and $\tau = 0.90$ (range 0.405--0.419).
Known Accuracy in the open-set setting (14--26\%) is lower than Experiment~1
because the prompt includes both known and unknown class labels, diluting the
softmax and causing more known images to fall below the threshold; it
represents the fraction of known images that were \emph{both} correctly
classified \emph{and} above $\tau$, and is not directly comparable to the
closed-set figure.
The False Rejection Rate rises monotonically. The limitation arises from the CLIP embedding space clustering all plant leaves
closely, as noted in prior work on open-set visual classification with dense
embedding models~\cite{dhamija2020openset}.
The threshold $\tau^* = 0.85$ was locked into the production pipeline prior
to Experiment~4 (qualitative pipeline evaluation).

\begin{figure}[h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{images/clip_threshold_sensitivity.png}
    \caption{CLIP confidence threshold sensitivity (mean of 5 runs).
             Known Accuracy (blue) declines as $\tau$ rises.
             Unknown Rejection Rate (green) rises monotonically.
             Unknown F1 (purple, dashed) peaks at $\tau^* = 0.85$ (red dotted line).}
    \label{fig:clip_threshold}
\end{figure}

\section{Experiment 3: Few-Shot Cosine Similarity Threshold}
\label{sec:exp3}

A cosine similarity threshold sweep from 0.70 to 0.94 was conducted for
the Few-Shot Engine, using a 5-shot support set of \textit{Squash Powdery
Mildew} images and \textit{Blueberry leaf} images as the negative class.
Results are averaged across 5 independent runs.

\begin{figure}[h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{images/few_shot_threshold_sensitivity.png}
    \caption{Cosine similarity threshold sensitivity for the Few-Shot Engine
             (single representative run). The optimal threshold $\tau = 0.76$
             maximises the mean F1-score (0.686) across 5 independent runs.}
    \label{fig:few_shot_threshold}
\end{figure}

\begin{table}[h]
    \centering
    \caption{Mean TPR, FPR, and F1 across 5 runs per threshold.
             Optimal threshold highlighted in bold.}
    \label{tab:few_shot_threshold}
    \begin{tabular}{cccc}
        \hline
        \textbf{Threshold ($\tau$)} & \textbf{Mean TPR (\%)} & \textbf{Mean FPR (\%)} & \textbf{Mean F1} \\
        \hline
        0.70 & 96.4 & 92.4 & 0.668 \\
        0.72 & 95.2 & 87.6 & 0.673 \\
        0.74 & 94.0 & 82.0 & 0.681 \\
        \textbf{0.76} & \textbf{90.4} & \textbf{73.2} & \textbf{0.686} \\
        0.78 & 86.4 & 66.8 & 0.683 \\
        0.80 & 81.2 & 59.6 & 0.674 \\
        0.82 & 78.0 & 54.4 & 0.671 \\
        0.84 & 72.0 & 48.4 & 0.653 \\
        0.86 & 63.2 & 41.2 & 0.618 \\
        0.88 & 55.2 & 35.6 & 0.578 \\
        0.90 & 41.6 & 26.0 & 0.494 \\
        0.92 & 30.0 & 14.8 & 0.407 \\
        0.94 & 14.0 &  5.2 & 0.227 \\
        \hline
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

F1 peaks at $\tau = 0.76$ (mean F1 = 0.686, TPR = 90.4\%, FPR = 73.2\%) and
collapses rapidly above $\tau = 0.86$.
The persistently high FPR across all thresholds reflects the proximity of
plant disease embeddings in the CLIP representation space and is acknowledged
as a key limitation.

\section{Experiment 4: Qualitative Pipeline Evaluation on PlantDoc}
\label{sec:exp4}

PlantDoc~\cite{DBLP:journals/corr/abs-1911-10317} contains 28 real-world disease categories
crawled from the Internet, with complex backgrounds, variable lighting, and
label noise --- an ideal stress test for end-to-end pipeline robustness.
The full DINO + CLIP pipeline was run on a sample of PlantDoc images:
Grounding DINO (SwinB) localises leaves and insects, CLIP classifies each
crop with open-set rejection ($\tau^* = 0.85$), and the LLM agent synthesises
detections into a structured \texttt{DiagnosisResult} with RAG retrieval and
self-correction.

\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\section{Contributions}

In this thesis, I developed an autonomous multimodal agent for diagnosing crop diseases, resolving the semantic gap between simple visual detection and providing actionable farming advice. I also take open-set perception and agent self-correction into account, which pushes beyond the limitations of traditional fixed-class AI systems in agriculture. I evaluated the pipeline extensively and achieved promising results. 

Compared to standard closed-set models, my system safely handles unknown crops by using a data-driven confidence threshold ($\tau^* = 0.85$, Section~\ref{sec:exp2}) and registers new diseases via a few-shot engine ($\tau = 0.76$, Section~\ref{sec:exp3}). Additionally, thanks to the self-correcting agent and crop-filtered ChromaDB retrieval layer, the system can dynamically enforce treatment safety rules (like avoiding pesticide conflicts) and eliminate LLM hallucinations. Thus, I believe this framework provides a strong trade-off between open-world flexibility and expert-level safety.

In conclusion, I hope that my contribution to multimodal agent architectures will inspire future research in building safe and reasoning-capable AI systems for agriculture.

\section{Future Work}

\paragraph{Improving Vision Foundation Models}
Despite the promising results of open-set vision, the ongoing false rejection rate ($\geq$13\% at $\tau=0.30$) and the few-shot false positive rate (73.2\% at $\tau=0.76$) suggest room for improvement. My future direction consists of incorporating specific negative examples, contrastive learning methods, or a simple classification layer on top of the frozen CLIP model to better scale to new crops. 

\paragraph{Benchmarking the Vision Pipeline Against State-of-the-Art Models}
While my current pipeline establishes a strong foundation, future research should focus on constructing a benchmark for the core vision components (Grounding DINO and CLIP) against other State-of-the-Art (SOTA) models. Because evaluating the entire reasoning agent is complex, comparing the vision pipeline's localization and classification performance on unstructured field datasets will help quantify the specific advantages of my open-set perception approach.

\paragraph{Expanded Coverage and Expert Validation}
The current CLIP label set primarily covers Tomato and Grape diseases. Expanding this to include a wider variety of crops and ingesting more diverse RAG documents would greatly increase the system's practical usefulness. Ultimately, testing with professional agronomists would confirm the value of the structured output and uncertainty warnings in real decision-making contexts.

\paragraph{Automated Dataset Annotation}
Because the vision pipeline combines zero-shot localization (Grounding DINO) with open-set classification (CLIP), it has significant potential to function as an auto-labeling tool. Future work could utilize this architecture to rapidly generate pseudo-labels and bounding boxes for large, unannotated agricultural image datasets, drastically reducing the manual labor required to train future supervised models.
